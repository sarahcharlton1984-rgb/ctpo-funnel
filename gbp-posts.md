# Google Business Profile pack

The highest intent traffic available to an accountancy practice, and the channel
with the lowest production cost. Someone typing "accountant for limited company"
is further down the funnel than anyone who will ever watch a Reel.

Most local practices have a neglected profile with a handful of reviews and no
posts. Twenty real reviews and a weekly post puts you ahead of nearly all of
them inside a month.

---

## Setup, once

1. **Claim and verify** the Tax 4 Pros profile if it is not already.
2. **Categories.** Primary: Accountant. Secondary: Tax preparation service,
   Tax consultant, Bookkeeping service.
3. **Services.** List them individually, because each one is a matchable term:
   limited company accounts, corporation tax returns, self assessment, payroll,
   VAT returns, bookkeeping, tax planning, company formation.
4. **The profile link goes to `/pay-calculator`, not the homepage.** A homepage
   asks them to browse. The calculator asks them one question and captures an
   email. Use the UTM: `?utm_source=gbp&utm_medium=profile&utm_campaign=link`
5. **Description.** Draft below.
6. **Hours, service area, photos.** Real photos, including one of Sarah. Profiles
   with faces get clicked more than profiles with logos.

## Reviews: the actual priority

Reviews move local ranking more than posts do, and they close people who are
comparing two firms. Twenty is the number to aim at first.

**Ask two clients a week, personally, one at a time.** Never a mass send. The
ask works when it is specific about what you helped with.

### Email ask

> Subject: A small favour
>
> {{first_name}},
>
> Quick one. We are trying to build up our Google reviews and yours would carry
> real weight, especially for other {{trade}} businesses looking for someone.
>
> If you have two minutes, the link is here: {{google_review_link}}
>
> If it helps, the thing worth mentioning is {{the specific thing you did for
> them}}. Anything honest is genuinely useful, good or otherwise.
>
> Thank you,
> Sarah

### Text ask

> {{first_name}}, would you mind leaving us a Google review? Takes two minutes
> and it makes a real difference to us: {{google_review_link}} Thanks. Sarah

### Rules

- Ask right after something went well. A completed year end, a saving found, a
  problem sorted.
- Never offer anything in exchange. It breaches Google's terms and it is
  obvious in the writing.
- Reply to every review within a day, including any negative one. A calm,
  specific reply to a complaint reads better to a prospect than a wall of
  five stars.

## Twelve weeks of posts

One a week. Roughly 200 to 300 characters plus a photo. Every one ends with a
link to `/pay-calculator` unless stated. Run each through `check_figures.py`
before posting, and re-check after any Budget.

**1.** Most directors have never been asked how much they actually need to take
out of the business. Not what the profit was. What they need. Answer that first
and the rest of the pay structure sorts itself out.

**2.** The salary and dividend split is a second order question. It matters, but
by the time profits are meaningful the bigger levers are pensions, retained
profit and timing. Nobody mentions those if nobody is looking.

**3.** Between £100,000 and £125,140 the personal allowance tapers away, which
creates an effective rate of around 60 percent. It is the most punishing band in
the UK system and it is avoidable if it is seen coming before the year end.

**4.** Changing accountants takes about a week and you do not have to tell your
old one yourself. The clearance letter does it. Most people who want to move
stay put because they imagine an awkward conversation that never happens.
→ link to `/changing-accountants`

**5.** If you have children and your income runs between roughly £60,000 and
£80,000, the High Income Child Benefit Charge is quietly clawing it back. Most
people affected have no idea it exists until the return is filed.

**6.** Ask any accountant what is NOT included in the fee. A firm with a genuinely
fixed price can answer in one sentence. If they cannot, that is the answer.
→ link to `/accountant-cost-limited-company`

**7.** Money taken out of the company and then lent straight back in is one of the
most expensive routine mistakes we see. If the business needs it, leaving it there
was always cheaper.

**8.** You can change accountants at any point in your financial year. Mid year is
usually better, because it leaves a planning window. Waiting for the year end
means the first year is pure catch up.

**9.** A firm that bills you for questions has trained you not to ask them. The
questions are where the money is, which makes that the most expensive line on
the invoice, and it never appears on the invoice.

**10.** Tax planning happens before your year end, not after it. If you only hear
from your accountant at the filing deadline, the window has already closed. Every
year.

**11.** We publish our prices, which is rarer in this profession than it should be.
Three fixed monthly plans, the list of what is included underneath each one, and
no invoice at the year end you were not expecting. → link to `/plans`

**12.** Your company setup was probably right when you started. Then you took on
staff, or profit doubled, or you bought a property, and nobody revisited it. The
structure that suits a one person startup rarely suits what you have now.

## After twelve weeks

Look at which posts produced profile clicks and calculator visits, and write the
next twelve in the shape of the ones that worked. The commercial intent posts
(4, 6, 11) and the specific number posts (3, 5) usually beat the general ones.
