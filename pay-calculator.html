How the numbers are built. Both routes extract the full profit in the year and count every layer of tax on it. The salary route pays the whole profit as salary: employer National Insurance at 15% above £5,000, employee National Insurance at 8% to £50,270 and 2% above, and income tax at 20% / 40% / 45% with the £12,570 personal allowance tapered above £100,000. The structured route pays a £12,570 salary, corporation tax on the rest (19% to £50,000, marginal relief to £250,000, then 25%), and dividend tax on the distribution at 10.75% / 35.75% / 39.35% with the £500 allowance. Assumes a sole director with no Employment Allowance, a standalone company with no associated companies and a 12 month accounting period, no other personal income, no student loan, England / Northern Ireland rates (Scottish salary bands differ), and no pension contributions, which are often the bigger lever and part of the real conversation. Retaining profit in the company instead of extracting it changes everything, which is also part of the real conversation. Figures are indicative and rounded, not tax advice.
