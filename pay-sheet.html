The Director's Pay Sheet 2026/27

The salary and dividend split at each profit level, every layer of tax counted. England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

The split that works for most directors

A salary of £12,570, then the rest as dividends. The salary uses your full personal allowance, so it carries no income tax and no employee National Insurance. The company pays about £1,136 employer's National Insurance on it, but the salary and that NI are both deductible against corporation tax, so the deduction is worth more than the NI costs at every corporation tax rate.

If the company pays other staff it may have the Employment Allowance to set against employer's NI, which makes the salary cheaper still. A company whose only employee paid above the threshold is its sole director cannot claim it, which is why the table below counts the full £1,136.

And the quiet reason £12,570 beats a token salary: it sits above the £6,708 lower earnings limit, so the year counts towards your State Pension. A £5,000 salary saves a little employer's NI and buys you no qualifying year at all.

The three lines that cost directors the most

£50,270. Total income past this point moves your dividends from 10.75% to 35.75%. The single most expensive threshold to drift over without noticing.
£60,000 to £80,000, if your household claims Child Benefit. You repay 1% of it for every £200 over £60,000, gone entirely by £80,000. A pension contribution reduces the income HMRC counts.
£100,000. Your personal allowance starts to withdraw, an effective rate of up to 60% on the slice to £125,140 depending on how the income is made up, and for parents the free childcare hours go at the same line. This is the strongest pension argument in the whole tax system.

You do not have to take it all out

The table assumes every pound of after tax profit is drawn. That is the worst case, and it is exactly where the planning starts. Profit you leave in the company stops at the corporation tax line. An employer pension contribution skips every line in the table. Which route is right depends on what the money is for, and that is a conversation, not a spreadsheet.
