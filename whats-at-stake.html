How the numbers are built. The model assumes one scenario: you sell the business, then your estate is assessed on the net proceeds. CGT uses 18% Business Asset Disposal Relief on the first £1m and 24% on the balance; it assumes you qualify for BADR (a 5%+ holding in a trading company, held two years, as an officer or employee) with the full £1m lifetime limit unused, and a negligible base cost. For a couple, only one BADR limit is modelled, so a couple splitting shares may do better. IHT uses the 2026/27 nil-rate band (£325,000 per person, frozen to April 2031), residence nil-rate band (£175,000 per person, tapered above £2m and lost by £2.35m single / £2.7m couple) and the 40% headline rate, applied to your estate plus the sale proceeds net of CGT. The residence band assumes a home worth at least that band passing to children or grandchildren. Business Property Relief, trusts and Family Investment Company routes are not modelled here, that is the point of the saveable range. The model is built for UK residents with their whole estate in UK IHT scope; if you are non resident or planning to leave, the scope rules change and your route is the application, not this page's numbers. Figures are indicative and rounded, not tax advice.
