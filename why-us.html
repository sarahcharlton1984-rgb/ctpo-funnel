Why directors switch to us

What we do, what moving actually involves, and who you would be dealing with.

Most accountants file your past. That is a real job and it has to be done properly. But filing your accounts and reducing your tax are two different pieces of work, and a lot of firms only ever do the first one.

Nobody sits down with you before the year end, while things can still be changed, and asks how much you actually need to take out of the business. So the year closes, the return gets filed, and you find out in January what it cost.

That is the gap we exist to close.

What you get every month

One fixed fee, agreed before we start, covering the compliance and the thinking. The filing happens quietly in the background and on time. The part you actually notice is that somebody looks at how you pay yourself once a year, before the year end rather than after it, and tells you plainly whether it still fits.

You can ask us a question without wondering what it will cost. That is included, because a firm that bills for questions trains you not to ask them, and the questions are where the money is.

Moving is the bit everyone dreads

It is also the reason most directors stay somewhere they have outgrown. So here is exactly what happens, start to finish.

  1. A fifteen minute callYou tell us what the business looks like. We tell you honestly whether we would make a difference. If we would not, we say so.
  2. You sign two thingsAn engagement letter and a form authorising us to act for you with HMRC. Both go through on your phone.
  3. We write to your current accountantThis is called professional clearance and it is routine. We ask for your records. You do not have to have an awkward conversation, and you do not have to explain yourself.
  4. We take over the records and the softwareBooks, prior year accounts, tax history, payroll. If you are on Xero or similar we pick it up as it is. If you are on spreadsheets we set you up properly.
  5. You are liveUsually inside a week of signing. Then we look at your pay structure, which for most people is the first time anyone has.
You can move at any point in your financial year. You do not have to wait for the year end, and waiting usually costs you a planning window rather than saving you anything.
